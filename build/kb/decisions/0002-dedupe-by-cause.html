What the queue shows

The chart uses Monday-to-Sunday weeks and distinct report IDs. Received and merged are different events. A fall in merges cannot establish a fall in incidents; keep tracking new reports and verify a fix against newly received reports.

Rule the desk can use while it keeps working

  1. Read the report body. Look up the tenant’s locale and form version in the tenant directory, and record the channel. Ask for missing facts rather than merging an unknown sender.
  2. A month/day swap through app or email, or a booking-v2 form, goes to the parser ticket for a day-first tenant. A booking-v3 form whose MM/DD/YYYY hint conflicts with the tenant’s day-first reader goes to the hint ticket. Confirm the described behavior agrees with the proposed route.
  3. Merge only when the affected path and failure mechanism match. Subject text can help search; it cannot decide whether two reports share a cause. The same subject can hide two bugs, and different subjects can describe one bug.
  4. Hold inconsistent or incomplete reports for investigation. In particular, a one-day move is not explained merely by a month/day classifier label. Keep the original report ID and link its investigation so it cannot vanish inside a larger ticket.
  5. Reconcile counts by unique report ID. Preserve raw intake tags and merge history as evidence, but report them separately from verified causes.

The same tenant can have different causes across channels; the same form channel can have different causes across tenants. Different subjects can share a cause. An unknown tenant does not establish a common cause. The classifier supports this check but does not read the body and cannot replace that step.
